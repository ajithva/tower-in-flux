Tower in Flux

The tower here isn't designed. It grows from simple local rules: each new block appears or doesn't depending on how many neighbours it has on the level below. Blocks with nothing under them fall, pile up as rubble, and can become ground for more building. Once the tower settles, the wind starts, and it gets stronger in random gusts.

Your goal: get the tower up to the target height, then keep it there through 3 gusts without building. Do that and you win.

How Tower in Flux works

The idea

Tower in Flux is a 3D cellular automaton that started as a Grasshopper script. Instead of drawing a tower, you set a few local rules and watch a form grow out of them. Then gravity and wind test how well it holds together.

How a level grows

Level 0 is scattered randomly (controlled by Density and Seed), and the centre block is always filled. Each new level looks at the 8 cells around each position on the level below, counting both tower blocks and rubble:

neighbours between Birth min and Birth max → block is born
block directly below and neighbours ≥ Survive min → block survives
otherwise → empty

With Pure birth on, only the birth rule applies, which gives sparser, more fragile towers.

The three phases

  • Growing: the tower is built one level at a time.
  • Collapsing: any block with nothing under it falls, unless it's held on both sides. Falling blocks drift outward and land as rubble, which piles a few blocks high.
  • Wind: the upper part of the tower (from 70% of the level count up) sways. Blocks with fewer neighbours sway more, and if one sways too far it breaks off and falls.

Gusts

While the wind blows, it picks up in random gusts every few seconds, each adding 10–30% more strength, up to ×3. Cubes visibly lean with the wind, and when one leans too far it snaps off and falls. Rubble piles stay put, but loose blocks on top can still be blown off. The wind gets stronger the higher you go, so every extra level catches more of it. Tall, thin and exposed blocks go first. Each new build halves the extra wind.

Stability. The tower behaves like a simple real structure. A floor's stiffness depends on where its cubes are: cubes far from the middle stiffen it most, like the frame of a tube skyscraper, so a hollow ring can be as strong as a solid block. Each floor also carries the wind on every floor above it, so wide bases and tapering tops help, while thin "necks" and top-heavy floors hurt. Gaps let some wind through, a cube resting on another cube is braced by it, cubes on an unbroken column from the ground (a spine) are anchored, and cubes with nothing directly beneath them are weaker. When edge cubes snap, the ones behind them are exposed next, so a tower erodes from the outside in. Exposed cubes also slowly wear out under repeated gusts.

The Stability meter rates the top of your tower: Solid (holds its height even in the strongest gusts, and never wears out), Steady (only the strongest gusts cost it levels) or Shaky (even mild gusts do), and names the shape it sees: tapered, tube, lattice, spine or top-heavy. A number like −4 means a gust would erode about 4 levels off the top, so build that much above the target or make the tower wider and stiffer.

Scoring

To win: reach the target height with a settled build, then hold it through 3 gusts in a row without building. The score card counts the gusts you've held. If a gust knocks the tower below the target, the count resets, and so does building while you're holding.

Points come only from your moves (New game or Build further) and from winning:

  • +100 per new level above your best height so far. Rebuilding lost height scores nothing.
  • +500 the first time a settled build reaches the target.
  • +2,000 for winning, plus 1,000 per move under the benchmark. The benchmark is the number of builds a good player should need: target ÷ levels per build, rounded up, + 1 (3 with the default settings).
  • After winning you can keep playing for a higher score, or start again.
  • Your best score is saved in this browser.

Tips

  • Wide, dense footprints (a high Density with Birth min around 3) stand up to wind much better than thin ones.
  • Build further only works during the Wind phase. It reseeds the chosen level and adds Levels more on top.
  • With Build on rubble on, the tower can spread onto the rubble around its base and brace itself.
  • Clear rubble takes away that support, and anything resting on it falls.

Lighting

Under Lighting & display, choose Day, Night or Cycle. In Cycle the sun crosses the sky, the moon and stars come out, and after dusk random cubes on the tower's sides light up like windows and switch on and off. Day length sets how long a full day takes, and Glow turns the bloom effect on or off. The lighting is purely visual and doesn't change the simulation.

Controls

Drag to orbit, scroll or pinch to zoom, and right-drag to pan. The Speed slider changes how fast the simulation runs.
